One-Point Perspective & Horizon Line

Horizon Line Vanishing Point Orthogonals Space

What It Is

One-point perspective uses a single vanishing point on the horizon line to make any surface receding directly away from the viewer — like a hallway, road, or train track — converge convincingly into deep space.

Why Artists Use It

It's the fastest way to make a flat drawing read as a real 3D space, and it's the foundation every other perspective system builds on.

How To Practice It — Step By Step

  1. 1
    Draw the horizon line

    A single horizontal line across your page at your eye level.

  2. 2
    Place the vanishing point

    Mark one point anywhere on the horizon line — everything receding into the distance will point toward it.

  3. 3
    Draw the front face

    Draw the front-facing surface of your object (a box, doorway, or building face) as a flat, undistorted rectangle.

  4. 4
    Draw the orthogonals

    Lightly connect each corner of that front face to the vanishing point — these guide lines show how the object recedes.

  5. 5
    Close the far edges

    Draw the back edges of the form parallel to the front face, stopping wherever feels right along the orthogonal lines.

  6. 6
    Clean up

    Erase the orthogonals you don’t need and darken the final edges.

Try This

Draw a simple hallway or a road lined with telephone poles — the poles get shorter and closer together as they approach the vanishing point.
