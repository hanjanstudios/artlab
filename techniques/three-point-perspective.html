Three-Point Perspective (Bird/Worm Eye)

Three-Point Perspective Low Level Horizon Atmospheric Value

What It Is

Three-point perspective adds a third vanishing point — above or below the horizon — so vertical lines also converge, creating a dramatic looking-up or looking-down view.

Why Artists Use It

It's what makes a skyscraper feel like it's towering over you, or a character feel small and vulnerable — the go-to setup for dramatic, cinematic compositions.

How To Practice It — Step By Step

  1. 1
    Choose your viewpoint

    Decide if you’re looking up (worm’s eye) or down (bird’s eye) at your subject.

  2. 2
    Place two vanishing points

    As in two-point perspective, put one on the horizon to the left and one to the right.

  3. 3
    Place the third vanishing point

    Far above the page if looking up, or far below it if looking down.

  4. 4
    Draw the nearest edge

    A single line angled slightly toward the third vanishing point, not perfectly vertical.

  5. 5
    Connect to all three points

    Build out each face of your form, converging lines toward all three vanishing points.

  6. 6
    Add atmospheric value

    Push far-away edges lighter and lower-contrast to sell the sense of extreme depth.

Try This

Sketch a tall building from street level looking straight up — notice how even the corners of the building lean inward as they rise.
