Technique Guide

Chiaroscuro & Extreme Tonal Shading

Chiaroscuro Value Ambient Light Cast Shadow

What It Is

Chiaroscuro (Italian for “light-dark”) pushes an extreme range between light and shadow, with very little gentle mid-tone gradation, to make forms feel sculptural and dramatic.

Why Artists Use It

It's the technique behind Caravaggio and Rembrandt's most famous work — a single controlled light source and deep, decisive shadow can make even a simple subject feel monumental.

How To Practice It — Step By Step

  1. 1
    Choose a single light source

    Decide its direction and keep it consistent across the whole piece.

  2. 2
    Block in three values only

    Lay down just light, midtone, and dark shapes first — resist blending until the big shapes are right.

  3. 3
    Push your darks further

    True chiaroscuro darks are much deeper than a beginner’s instinct — don’t be afraid of pure black.

  4. 4
    Add cast shadows

    Every object throws a shadow with a hard edge nearest itself, softening as it moves away.

  5. 5
    Reserve your brightest highlight

    Save the lightest value for only one or two focal points so it stays the eye’s focus.

  6. 6
    Blend selectively

    Save smooth blending for curved surfaces; keep hard edges wherever a surface changes direction sharply.

Try This

Light a simple object (an apple, a mug) with a single desk lamp in an otherwise dark room and draw only what you see — no imagined detail in the shadow side.
