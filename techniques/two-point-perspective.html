Two-Point Perspective (Corner View)

Two-Point Perspective Picture Plane High Horizon

What It Is

Two-point perspective uses two vanishing points, both on the horizon line, to draw an object from its corner — showing two receding faces at once instead of one flat front.

Why Artists Use It

It's how you draw most real-world objects and buildings, since we rarely view things dead-on. It reads as far more dynamic and spatially convincing than a one-point setup.

How To Practice It — Step By Step

  1. 1
    Set up the horizon

    Draw the horizon line, then place two vanishing points on it — one far to the left, one far to the right of your page.

  2. 2
    Draw the near corner

    Draw a single vertical line — this is the nearest corner of your object, closest to the viewer.

  3. 3
    Connect to both vanishing points

    From both ends of that vertical line, lightly draw guide lines out to each vanishing point.

  4. 4
    Set the width of each face

    Add a second vertical line on each side to define how wide each of the two visible faces is.

  5. 5
    Complete the far edges

    Connect the tops and bottoms of those new verticals back toward the vanishing points to close each face.

  6. 6
    Refine

    Darken the true edges of your form and erase the extra construction lines.

Try This

Draw a simple box or building corner first before attempting anything complex — get comfortable finding both vanishing points before adding detail.
